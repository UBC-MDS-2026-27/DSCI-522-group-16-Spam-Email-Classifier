# Community Code of Conduct

## Community

We want a productive, welcoming, and happy community that can consider new ideas, improve our project, and foster collaboration among people with different needs, interests, skills, and backgrounds.

We gain strength from diversity and actively seek participation from people who enhance it. This Code of Conduct exists to help diverse groups collaborate with mutual respect and benefit. We will challenge prejudice that could jeopardize anyone's participation in the project.

This Code of Conduct governs how we behave when working on or representing the Spam Email Classifier project, both in public and private. We expect it to be honored by all contributors and maintainers, including people who represent the project officially or informally, claim affiliation with it, or participate directly.

We strive to:

### Be considerate

Our work will be used by other people, and we depend on the work of others. Every decision may affect users and colleagues, so we consider those impacts before acting.

### Be respectful

Disagreement is no excuse for poor manners. We work together to resolve conflict, assume good intentions, and act with empathy. We do not allow frustration to become a personal attack or permit harassment, intimidation, discrimination, or threats. A community where people feel uncomfortable or unsafe is not a productive one.

### Take responsibility for our words and actions

We may make mistakes. When we do, we acknowledge them, listen carefully to people who have been harmed or offended, and work to repair the harm.

### Be collaborative

Our project is a complex whole made of many parts and perspectives. Collaboration is essential to connect those parts, reduce duplication, and improve the quality of our work. We celebrate good collaboration and coordinate efforts wherever possible. We prefer transparency and involvement of interested participants as early as practical.

### Be generous

We share knowledge, credit, feedback, and reusable work whenever appropriate. We believe that collaboration and open exchange can benefit other contributors and users.

### Value decisiveness, clarity, and consensus

Social and technical disagreements are normal, but we do not allow them to persist without resolution. Participants should seek constructive agreement. When consensus is not possible, we bring the issue to the project maintainers so they can arbitrate and provide clarity.

### Ask for help when unsure

Nobody is expected to be perfect. Asking questions early helps prevent problems, and questions are encouraged. Those who are asked should respond in a timely and helpful manner.

### Step down considerately

When someone leaves or disengages from the project, we ask that they do so in a way that minimizes disruption. They should communicate their departure and take reasonable steps so others can continue their work.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is unacceptable in project spaces or when someone represents the project:

- Harassment, bullying, intimidation, stalking, or threats
- Discrimination or exclusion based on age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of experience, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, sexual identity or orientation, or other protected characteristic
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention or advances
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, or personal or political attacks
- Publishing another person's private information without explicit permission
- Sustained disruption of discussions, meetings, issues, or pull requests
- Deliberate misrepresentation of another person's work or contributions
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern or participates in an investigation
- Other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within project repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, meetings, chat channels, presentations, and other project spaces. It also applies when an individual is representing the project publicly, such as through an official project email address or social media account.

## Leadership, authority, and responsibility

We all lead by example, in debate and in action. We encourage contributors to take responsibility and participate in improving the project. Leadership is not limited to a title: it is a responsibility that anyone may exercise by taking constructive action.

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing this Code of Conduct. They should gather relevant perspectives and data, support teams in reaching decisions, and provide a clear direction when consensus is not practical.

We recognize that decisions may not always be perfect. We prefer to make informed decisions, learn from their outcomes, and improve future decisions rather than postpone action indefinitely. Contributors may raise concerns or seek review when they disagree with a decision, but once maintainers have reached and documented a decision within their authority, they should work together to implement it.

### Teamwork and credit

A leader's foremost goal is the success of the team. Leaders should know when to act, when to step back, and when to delegate or take responsibility themselves.

Leaders should celebrate team members' work and give appropriate credit. Good leadership makes others more capable and visible rather than seeking personal attention.

### Courage and considerateness

Leadership may require difficult or unpopular decisions. Such decisions should be bold enough to move the project forward, but considerate of the people affected. Leaders should communicate changes and their reasoning clearly and early.

### Conflicts of interest

Leaders and maintainers must disclose and manage conflicts arising from employment or other projects. When a conflict could affect a decision, they should abstain or delegate it to someone able to act impartially. When in doubt, a leader should ask for a second opinion.

## Reporting and enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, discriminatory, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be reported by contacting the project team at audreyt2@student.ubc.ca. Reports may also instead be sent privately to a current project maintainer.

Reports should include, where possible:

- A description of the incident
- The date and location
- Names or descriptions of people involved
- Relevant messages, links, or other evidence
- Any immediate safety or follow-up concern

Project maintainers will review and investigate reports promptly, fairly, and as consistently as possible. They will respect reporters' privacy and keep personal information confidential except when disclosure is necessary to investigate or address an incident or meet a legal obligation.

Possible consequences include:

1. A private warning explaining which behavior was inappropriate
2. A request for a public or private apology
3. Temporary restriction from project discussion or collaboration
4. Removal or rejection of an offending contribution
5. A temporary ban from project spaces
6. A permanent ban from project spaces

The response will depend on the severity, impact, and repetition of the behavior. Maintainers may use a combination of these consequences and should document decisions internally. Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this Code of Conduct in good faith may face temporary or permanent consequences determined by the remaining project leadership.
